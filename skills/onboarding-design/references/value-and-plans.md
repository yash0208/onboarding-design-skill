# Value communication and paid-plan fit

## Make benefits concrete

Use this chain: **user problem → product action → visible output → useful consequence → evidence**.

For a fictional writing product: “Turn your notes into an editable first draft” explains the mechanism and output. “Write 10× faster” does not, unless a suitable study supports it. The product should demonstrate the output early, with examples clearly distinguished from the user's real result.

Differentiate a promise, a demonstrated capability, and a measured result. Show relevant limits: quality depends on input, imports may need review, generated work still needs judgment. Do not imply guaranteed jobs, revenue, grades, health outcomes, or other outcomes outside the product's control.

## Claims ladder

| Evidence available | Appropriate claim | What not to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified feature only | Describe the task it performs and the result | Invent hours saved |
| Observed user difficulty | Explain the problem and propose a benefit hypothesis | Claim general demand from one anecdote |
| Timed comparable task study | Report context, sample, metric and uncertainty | Generalize to all users or ignore output quality |
| Controlled product experiment | Report the tested cohort, variant, period and effect uncertainty | Treat signup lift as retention or causation across products |

For a time-saved study, define equivalent starting inputs, output-quality criteria and completion events. Include setup, waiting, review and correction. Measure baseline and assisted tasks, account for learning/order effects, and report distribution plus failures—not only the fastest successful user. A possible estimate is median baseline time minus median assisted time; do not present a pooled median difference as an individual guarantee. An ROI calculator must expose assumptions, use user-supplied inputs and label estimates. Time saved is not automatically money earned or productivity gained.

## Signup positioning

Match the entry promise to what the user can actually do next. Explain why an account is needed, and disclose important restrictions before asking for effort. Preserve intended destinations through authentication. Where identity is optional and safe, consider delaying signup until saving or sharing value; where it is required, keep the legitimate gate and reduce unnecessary profile questions. Ecommerce findings are not universal SaaS rules [R03](research.md).

## Plan-fit decision

First read the actual plan catalog, entitlements, pricing currency/cadence, trial behavior and cancellation flow. If these are unknown, mark the decision unresolved—do not invent a free tier or trial. If no paid plans exist, omit monetization screens.

| User state | Helpful behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Exploring with adequate current access | Help complete the task; make plan information discoverable |
| Needs a genuinely paid capability | Explain what unlocks, relevant limits, exact cost and alternatives |
| At a real usage limit | Explain limit/reset and preserve work; show available options |
| Existing subscriber or invited member | Honor entitlement; offer sign-in/restore or admin path where appropriate |
| Trial nearing its end | Explain actual end date, renewal behavior, charge and management path |

Benefits may be explained during onboarding; a mandatory purchase screen is not automatically appropriate. Trigger offers from a relevant need, not assumed income or vulnerability. Do not add repeated blocking upsells, false countdowns, preselected add-ons or guilt-based exits. Make an offered free/limited path understandable, but do not create one the business does not offer.

A plan comparison shows the actual billing total and period prominently, meaningful feature/usage differences, introductory and subsequent charges, and access after cancel/downgrade. Explain when payment starts and how to manage it. For credits, disclose cost before the action, what consumes credits, and verified retry/refund behavior. Do not equate an available credit balance with consent to spend it.

Use current provider/platform guidance for the implementation [R11–R12](research.md), and obtain appropriate review for applicable subscription/consumer rules. This skill is a design aid, not a legal compliance certification.

## Evaluate fit rather than pressure

Measure paid conversion alongside retained usage, task success, cancellations/refunds, billing confusion and support demand. Segment by relevant user intent and acquisition channel. A variant that raises purchases through misunderstanding is not a successful onboarding design.
